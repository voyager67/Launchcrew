# Research brief workflow

1. Translate the question into decision-relevant subquestions and define the date range or version context.
2. Prefer primary/official sources for product documentation, specifications, standards, release notes, papers, and policy. Use independent sources to cross-check claims or provide context.
3. Record source URL, publisher, publication/update date, access date when relevant, claim supported, and caveat.
4. Compare alternatives against explicit criteria. Do not blend incomparable metrics or treat vendor claims as independent verification.
5. For current facts, check freshness and note whether a source describes publication date or event date. Flag stale, undated, or conflicting evidence.
6. Separate facts, interpretation, assumptions, and recommendations. Quote sparingly; paraphrase faithfully.
7. State what evidence would change the conclusion. Avoid collecting extra facts that do not affect the user's decision.

## Output

- Executive answer and recommendation
- Findings by subquestion
- Comparison table with criteria and evidence
- Sources with dates and claim mapping
- Limitations / conflicting evidence
- Decision implications and next step

## Guardrails

Never fabricate citations, source details, direct quotes, benchmarks, or review sentiment. If web access or a source is unavailable, say so. For high-impact decisions, recommend specialist review when evidence or jurisdiction-specific expertise is insufficient.
